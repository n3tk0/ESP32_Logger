Why it's like this
5 notes

No seconds. The panel repaints on a timer measured in minutes, so a seconds field would be wrong the moment it was drawn.

Wording. The browser page and the Kindle's own FBInk panel together — they are one design drawn twice, and nobody reads one in English and the other in Bulgarian. Both wordings are in the firmware, so this needs no reflash. It does not translate the names you have given your places: those are your text and are drawn as you typed them. The weather provider's own summary is English whichever way this is set.

Typeface. It depends on its firmware. Every choice falls back to a generic family, so a face the device does not carry renders in the browser's default rather than in nothing.

What a reload costs. Every reload repaints the whole panel, which flashes and costs battery. Follow data predicts when the next reading is due and reloads right after it, so the page is never showing a number it could already have replaced. Clock sync aligns the reload to the minute boundary, so the displayed time changes when the minute does. This is the browser page's cadence — the FBInk panel keeps its own, in dash.conf on the reader.

What standalone means. A collector running as its own access point — WiFi and ESP-NOW nodes talking straight to it, nothing upstream — cannot fetch a forecast, and the forecast band is an eighth of the page. On the standalone page that band is gone and the readings take it: the same eleven places, a sixth larger, with the chart and the week strip where they were. Follow the collector switches by itself: it draws the standalone page while this device is an access point, while it was built without the forecast module, or while nothing has been able to refresh the forecast for six hours. The other two settings fix the shape whatever the network is doing — which is how you see one of them from a desk. The readings grow by a sixth and not by a third because the freed band is vertical: every number is limited by the column it sits in, and "1008 hPa" three across already fills 87 px of a 90 px cell.

The page, zone by zone

Every region of the e-ink page, in the order it is drawn. Open one — or tap it in the panel beside this — and everything about it is in one place: whether it is drawn, whether its figures are heavy, and which reading fills it.

Shown takes a region off the page entirely and gives its height back to the ones above it, which is for the reader who wants two numbers and nothing else. Bold sets its figures heavier — for a shelf far enough away that size alone stops being enough.

Clock

Drawn only by the dated clock style.

The FBInk panel draws its clock from the Kindle's own time, in this collector's time zone, and sets that time from this collector's this often. Unless this is Never, a clock found more than ten minutes out is set at once. dash.conf's CLOCK_SYNC on the Kindle wins over this.

Week strip

The forecast takes the calendar's place and height: today and the six days after it, each with its condition and its high and low. While it is there, the forecast band's columns step through the next hours. It needs the Weather forecast module and a fresh forecast; without one the calendar is drawn. The week strip itself is switched on and off in the Zones tab.

Dividing lines

The lines between the sections and between the two columns, on the browser page and the FBInk panel alike. The design pixel is scaled to the panel, so Thin is one pixel on a 600 x 800 Kindle and two on a 1072 x 1448 one. The line under the clock is drawn one step lighter.

Size of the readings

As large as fits sets each reading as large as its room allows, but never larger than the headline, so one reading alone fills its row. The others are that size less a tenth at a time, for more white around them. Three indoor readings are drawn as two columns: the first large, the other two one above the other beside it.

Units and language

The three-hour change follows the same unit.

Whole degrees set the big numerals wider apart.

Typeface

Written straight into the page's stylesheet, so it is filtered down to letters, digits, spaces, commas, quotes and hyphens. End it with a generic family.

How often the reader reloads

The reader

It decides the chart's dimensions and the icon sizes served by /kindle/data. Ask the reader opens /kindle/probe, which answers with what the reader actually is — rather than leaving it to be guessed from a list.

These two feed the chart and the 24-hour history. Which reading stands in which place is chosen zone by zone in the Zones tab.

The desk page's chart keeps two lines; each can follow one of the places. A place with nothing in it draws the default instead. Two different metrics are drawn to two scales, the dashed line's values down the right.

The wall page puts the outdoor reading across the whole width, the grid and the indoor readings in two columns under it, and the clock beside the forecast at the foot. The chart and the week strip are left off. Upright only: turned, the page is the desk one on its side. The panel and the browser page are set apart; the preview shows the panel.

90° and 270° draw the landscape page: the clock and the week across the top, the readings beside the chart, five forecast columns. The FBInk panel can also be turned on the Kindle itself (KUAL → Settings → Screen), which wins over this. The browser page can be turned on its own, or follow the panel; the preview shows the panel.

The browser page is laid out at the reader's own screen size, in the browser's pixels. Auto asks the browser once and reloads at that size; a reader that runs no script gets the one chosen here. Several readers at once: open /kindle/probe on each and bookmark the /kindle?scr=… address it shows. Browser bar is the height the browser keeps for its own address bar; if the bottom of the page is cut off, raise it (about 110 on a Paperwhite 4).

Update the reader's scripts

Upload esp32dash-kindle-<version>.tar from the repository's Releases and press Offer to the readers. Every reader running a newer script picks it up on its next fetch, checks it, installs it over itself and restarts — its settings stay. A reader on a script from before this needs one install by hand.
