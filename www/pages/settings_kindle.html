The page, zone by zone

Every region of the e-ink page, in the order it is drawn. Open one — or tap it in the panel beside this — and everything about it is in one place: whether it is drawn, whether its figures are heavy, and which reading fills it.

Shown takes a region off the page entirely and gives its height back to the ones above it, which is for the reader who wants two numbers and nothing else. Bold sets its figures heavier — for a shelf far enough away that size alone stops being enough.

Why there are no seconds

The panel repaints on a timer measured in minutes, so a seconds field would be wrong the moment it was drawn.

What the wording does and does not cover

The browser page and the Kindle's own FBInk panel together — they are one design drawn twice, and nobody reads one in English and the other in Bulgarian. Both wordings are in the firmware, so this needs no reflash.

It does not translate the names you have given your places: those are your text and are drawn as you typed them. The weather provider's own summary is English whichever way this is set.

Typeface

Written straight into the page's stylesheet, so it is filtered down to letters, digits, spaces, commas, quotes and hyphens. End it with a generic family.

Which faces a reader can actually reach

It depends on its firmware. Every choice falls back to a generic family, so a face the device does not carry renders in the browser's default rather than in nothing.

How often the reader reloads

What a reload costs

Every reload repaints the whole panel, which flashes and costs battery. Follow data predicts when the next reading is due and reloads right after it, so the page is never showing a number it could already have replaced. Clock sync aligns the reload to the minute boundary, so the displayed time changes when the minute does.

This is the browser page's cadence. The FBInk panel keeps its own, in dash.conf on the reader.
